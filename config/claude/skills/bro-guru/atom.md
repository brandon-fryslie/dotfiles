# The Atom — the substance unit

Every sellable thing in the product is made of atoms. One atom is one transformation, in four beats. Master the atom and the whole product is a crank: a lead magnet is one atom dressed for the open web; a course module is one atom with exercises; an email is one atom in 200 words; a thread is one atom truncated to a hook. **Same unit, different arrangements** — which is why all content shares this one spec.

## The four beats

1. **PAIN — the scar, in their words.** Open on a pain the reader has *already felt*, phrased the way they'd phrase it to a friend, not the way an expert would diagnose it. If you can't name the scar, you don't have an atom yet — stop. The pain is the hook and the qualifier: the right reader thinks "that's literally me."

2. **NAME — the true fundamental.** Give the pain its real name. This is the gift the grifter can't give, because they don't know it. The reader has felt this forever and never had a word for it; the word is the relief. Name it honestly — the name must be *true*, traceable to something you can defend (for swe4vibe, a `[LAW:token]`; for other subjects, a real principle, not a coined buzzword).

3. **MODEL — the one idea that dissolves it.** The single mental model that makes the pain stop being mysterious. Not five tips — *one* model, stated so plainly they can repeat it back. Minimum effective dose: strip the academic ceremony, keep the load-bearing idea. If you're explaining more than one thing, it's two atoms — split them.

4. **PROOF — a before/after they can feel.** Make the transformation tangible on *their* world, today. A concrete before (the broken thing) and after (the fixed thing) they could run, see, or do themselves. Felt, not asserted. "Here's the swamp; here's the same thing after the model; notice it can't break the old way anymore." Proof is what turns a nice idea into a win they'll pay to repeat.

## Quality bar (an atom is done when all hold)

- **It traces to truth.** The NAME and MODEL are real and defensible — not invented to sound smart. If challenged by an expert, they survive.
- **It's one thing.** One pain, one name, one model. Needing "and" means it's two atoms.
- **The proof is on their world, not a toy.** A slightly ugly real before/after beats a clean fictional one. The audience can smell fiction.
- **It meets them where they are.** No purity test, no "you should have done it right the first time." The atom adds a tool; it never confiscates the one that got them here.
- **The free atom is whole.** When an atom is the lead magnet, all four beats are delivered, ungated. No cliffhanger on the part that matters. The give is the proof the whole product is real.

## The anti-atom (reject on sight)

- A tip list with no underlying model ("10 ways to…") — no NAME, no MODEL, nothing transferable.
- A NAME with no PROOF — a vocabulary lesson the reader can't use.
- A PROOF on a fictional/toy example the reader can't map to their own mess.
- Pain the reader hasn't actually felt — you're teaching your interests, not solving theirs.
- Two-plus models crammed in — they'll remember none. Split.
