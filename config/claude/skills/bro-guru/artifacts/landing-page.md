# Artifact: Landing / Sales Page

> One of four capped channel templates. This one owns the **conversion copy**, not the visual design. For the look, hand off to the `saas-landing-page` skill (or the product's own design system) — this template produces the copy blocks it fills. Separation of concerns: this owns *what it says*, that owns *how it looks*.

**What it is:** the page that turns an interested visitor (usually arriving from the lead magnet) into a buyer. It's the **offer ladder** made visible, wrapped in the promise and the proof. Built from the profile — every block below is a profile field, rendered.

## Copy blocks (top to bottom)

1. **Hero — the promise.** The From→To, big and blunt. One line that names where they are and where they'll be. Subhead: who it's for + the one-sentence "how." A single primary CTA button. No feature soup up top — sell the transformation, not the syllabus.

2. **The enemy.** Two or three sentences naming the false belief / dreaded path. Give them a side. ("You don't need a CS degree. You don't need to start over. You need the five things nobody told you.")

3. **The proof / free win.** Surface the lead magnet right here — "don't trust me, here's a whole lesson free, go fix something with it, then come back." This block is the credibility engine; it's the move competitors can't copy.

4. **The curriculum as outcomes.** The atom list, but each atom titled as the **scar it kills**, not as a topic. "Module 3: Why it works then randomly breaks — gone." They're buying the disappearance of specific pains they recognize from the list. Let the list be long; the length *is* the value signal.

5. **Who this is / isn't for.** A short two-column qualifier. Naming who it's *not* for builds more trust than another benefit — it proves you're not selling to everyone.

6. **The offer ladder.** Tiers, cheapest to dearest, with the recommended one visually anchored. Same substance per rung, more access as you climb. Price stated plainly.

7. **Objection killers.** 3–5 of the real objections this audience holds ("I don't have time," "I've bought courses before and bounced," "the AI will just do this for me soon"), answered honestly and briefly. Honest answers beat clever ones here.

8. **Final CTA.** Restate the promise, restate the cost of not acting (real, not fake-scarcity), one button. Close confident.

## Gate (plus `voice.md`'s)

- Does the hero sell a **transformation**, not a feature list?
- Is the **free win** present and genuinely free?
- Are atoms titled as **scars killed**, not topics?
- Is every price/claim/scarcity statement **literally true**?
- Exactly **one offer**, one primary action repeated — not a maze of choices.
